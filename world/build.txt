# THE GRAMMAR LADDER (engine/build.js): a sentence grown from the simplest one, ONE GRAMMAR RULE A STEP, every step a
# whole right sentence -- Der Hund schläft. -> Der alte Hund schläft. -> Der große alte Hund schläft. -> Der große alte
# Hund schläft heute. -> Der große alte Hund schläft heute im Garten. No story, no scene: grammar and the words'
# relations alone -- the verb's frame and the categories its slots take (world/meaning), what an adjective describes,
# the order of adjectives (world/adjorder.txt), the everyday verbs and the manner adverbs they take (world/lists.txt:
# verb.routine, manner.rád, manner.*), the places below. Language-free: each language's realizer says every step, and
# <lang>/rules/notes.txt says how that language does each structure the step shows (the rule).
#
# THE BASE: an everyday verb (world/lists.txt verb.routine, manner.rád; one word, not a phrase) with its frame, at most
# one object (a patient), its subject a noun (so adjectives have something to describe: SUBJECTS below mostly) -- or, now and then,
# `noun + be + adjective` (Der Hund ist alt.). The words: the commoner the likelier, by their rank (order.tsv).
#
# THE STEPS, in the order a ladder takes them (a ladder takes some, each where it fits):
#   STEP name {weight} -- what it adds
# The structures a step shows are not declared: they are read off the plan (engine/structures.js) -- adding a place to
# a clause with a time shows both "place-basic" and "adverb-order".
#   adjective        an adjective that describes a noun (the subject, the object) -- one of world/adjorder.txt mostly
#   adjective-order  a second adjective of another kind (each language says the two in its own order: <lang>/rules/adjectives.txt)
#   degree           a degree word on an adjective (DEGREE below), never on one of adj.ungradable
#   possessive       a person's own thing: my, her ... (POSSESSIVE below) on a noun the subject does not name
#   plural           the subject in the plural (a countable noun)
#   comparison       (a predicate adjective) the comparative, than a noun it describes too
#   past perfect future   the tense (one of them)
#   modal            a modal verb (MODAL below) -- in the present, its subject a person or an animal
#   negation         not -- an object with a/an becomes no/kein (no-det)
#   frequency        how often (FREQUENCY below) -- an everyday verb's; not beside a time
#   time             when (TIME below, by the tense)
#   manner           how: an adverb whose world/lists.txt manner.* list has the verb (gladly: in the present, no modal)
#   place            where (PLACE below: the places the verb is done at)
#   yes-no           the question
#   wh               a question word: where (for the place), when (for the time), else where
#   and              a second main clause: another everyday action of the same subject (a pronoun)
#   because although a reason, a concession: the subject in a state (adj.state) that describes it
#   relative         a relative clause on the subject: an everyday action of it (its frame's subject)
