# HOW ENGLISH DOES EACH GRAMMAR STRUCTURE (engine/build.js: the grammar ladder shows the note of every structure a step
# brings; the names are world/syllabus.txt's). `structure = the rule, with a short example`.
present = Subject – verb – object, always in this order; -s in the 3rd person singular: The dog sleeps. An action going on now: is sleeping.
predicate = be + an adjective or a noun: The dog is old.
object = The object follows the verb directly, nothing between them: She reads a book.
adjective = The adjective stands before the noun and never changes: an old dog, the old dogs.
adjective-order = Several adjectives follow opinion – size – quality – age – shape – colour – origin – material, with no comma between them: a lovely big old red German car.
one-gladly-very = very, really, quite stand right before the adjective: very old. "Gladly" is said with like + -ing: She likes dancing.
possessive = my, your, his, her, our, their replace the article: my dog.
comparison = Short adjectives + -er (older), long ones more + adjective (more expensive), then than: older than the cat.
past = Regular verbs -ed (played), irregular ones their own form (slept); questions and negation with did: Did he sleep? He did not sleep.
perfect = have/has + past participle – for the past with a link to now; not with a finished time like yesterday (then the simple past): She has read it. She read it yesterday.
future = will + infinitive: The dog will sleep.
modal = can, must, want to + infinitive; can and must take no to: She can swim. She wants to swim.
negation = do/does/did + not before the verb (or not after be, a modal, have in the perfect): He does not sleep. He is not old.
no-det = "no" before the noun, or not ... a/any: I have no dog = I do not have a dog.
frequency = always, often, sometimes stand before the main verb, after be: She often reads. She is always tired.
time-basic = on + day (on Saturday), in + month, at + hour (at eight o'clock); time usually goes at the END, or at the very beginning.
time-phrases = last week, next week, every day – no preposition.
manner = The manner adverb (-ly: quickly, quietly) goes after the verb and its object: She speaks the language quietly.
place-basic = in the garden, at home, at the beach; place goes after the object.
adverb-order = At the end of the clause: manner – place – time: She sang loudly in the park yesterday.
yes-no = do/does/did first (or be, a modal, have): Does the dog sleep? Is it old? Can it swim?
wh-basic = The question word, then do/does/did (or be, a modal) and the subject: Where does the dog sleep?
coord = and, but, or join two clauses: She reads and he cooks.
that-because-if = because, that, if: normal word order in the clause; no comma when it follows the main clause: He stays home because he is ill.
although-so-that = although, so that: normal word order; a comma before although: She reads, although she is tired.
relative = who for people, that/which for things, right after the noun: The dog that plays in the garden sleeps.
