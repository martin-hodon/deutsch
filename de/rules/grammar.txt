# THE GRAMMAR (sentences/README.md): how a German sentence is put together -- a weighted context-free grammar over the
# FIELDS of the German clause, read by trainer/grammar.js. What fills the fields is a clause's PLAN (trainer/
# sentences.js: the verb and its frame, the subject and objects meaning-checked, the tense, the negation...); the
# forms come from the morphology. A line:
#
#     LHS -> symbol symbol ...   {weight}        (alternatives: more lines with the same LHS; weight 1 if left out;
#                                                 {1/2/3}: a weight for each COMPLEXITY, simple to rich)
#
# a symbol is
#     Name        another rule
#     role        lowercase: a part of the plan -- `subj`, `vfin`, `obj.A`... -- written where it stands; a role used
#                 once in a clause is not repeated (moved to the front, asked for by a question word, it is not in
#                 the middle field too); a role the plan does not have is left out
#     w.word      that dictionary word itself, spoken (w.entweder, w.je)
#     "text"      itself: punctuation
#
# AGREEMENT is carried by the roles: the subject's person and number make the finite verb's form; a noun phrase's
# case (its role), gender and number make its determiner's, adjectives' and noun's forms; the declension (strong,
# weak, mixed) follows from the determiner; a relative pronoun takes its noun's gender and number and its own case.

# ---- THE SYLLABUS ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# The grammar in the order it is learnt (the levels A1-B2 of the Common European Framework, as German courses teach
# them), each feature with its stage: a sentence made at a stage (loadSentences({ stage: "A2.1" })) uses only the
# features up to it; a rule below tagged @feature only once that feature is unlocked. What a feature means is the
# planner's (trainer/sentences.js asks has(feature)); the examples are sentences the rules build (sentences/tests.txt).
#
# A1.1 -- the first sentences: a statement, a question, the present
# A1.2 -- objects, commands, modals
# A2.1 -- the perfect, the dative, the first subordinate clauses
# A2.2 -- the past of sein and haben, polite forms, more questions
# B1.1 -- every tense, relative clauses, infinitives
# B1.2 -- the passive, paired conjunctions, the past Konjunktiv II
# B2.1 -- every passive, reported speech, the double infinitive, participles
# B2.2 -- the written language: extended attributes, nominal style

# the rules that are CLAUSES: each has a plan of its own (a verb, its subject and objects, a tense...)
CLAUSES: MAIN INVERTED SUB REL YESNO WH IMP ZU REP DROP ZUADV JE DESTO CONDV WERREL

# the rules that must write something: the front field is never empty (no object to front: the subject goes there)
FILLED: FRONT

# the WORDS that fill a role of their own: the conjunctions joining two main clauses (`coord`), starting a subordinate
# clause (`conj`; `conj.past`: only with a clause in the past -- als), and an object clause's (by its frame: dass, ob)
# (and aber only for a contrast the planner can make: one of the two clauses negated; no oder, denn: they want a
#  link no rule knows)
# (denn: like weil -- a state that explains; and the two-word ones for a dropped subject: Er kauft Brot und trinkt Kaffee)
# ... a ROUTINE: when, before, as soon as, if, while, until (Bevor ich gehe, ...; Ich warte, bis er kommt)
# (wenn first -- A2; the others with B1)
# ... B2: the manner (indem), the result (sodass), what does not happen (ohne dass, anstatt dass), as if (als ob + Konj. II)
# ... since (a sentence about now: Seit er hier wohnt, ...), a purpose (damit er schlafen kann)
# ... an infinitive's (Ich spare, um ein Auto zu kaufen; Er geht, ohne zu grüßen)
# ... a clause saying a state: explaining a negated or routine main clause (Er kommt nicht, weil er krank ist), or
# against any (Er arbeitet, obwohl er müde ist)
# ... the past only (als), and nachdem: its clause earlier than the main one (pluperfect in the past: Nachdem er
# gegessen hatte, ging er; perfect now: Nachdem er gegessen hat, geht er)
# PREPOSITIONS that take a name, not a noun with an article, for a place (nach Berlin, nach Polen -- "in die Stadt",
# not "nach der Stadt")
WORDS prep.names: nach
# ... and those that take no country or town (zu: to a person, a building -- not "zur Ukraine": in die Ukraine)
WORDS prep.no-towns: zu
# DEGREE words before an adjective (ein sehr großes Haus, Er ist zu müde) -- and the adjectives no degree goes with
# MODAL PARTICLES, as spoken: in a command (Komm doch mal!), a question (Was machst du denn?), a statement (Das ist
# ja schön), about what will be (Das wird schon gehen)
WORDS part.imp: mal {2} | doch mal {2} | doch {1} | bitte {2}
WORDS part.bitte: bitte
WORDS part.question: denn
WORDS part.statement: ja {2} | doch {1} | eben {0.5} | halt {0.5}
WORDS part.future: schon
# NEGATIONS beyond nicht: no longer (present, past), not yet (present, perfect), not at all
WORDS neg.longer: nicht mehr
WORDS neg.yet: noch nicht
WORDS neg.all: gar nicht
# PAIRED conjunctions for two objects (sowohl Kaffee als auch Tee; weder Brot noch Milch; nicht nur ..., sondern auch ...;
# entweder ... oder ...)
# verbs whose perfect takes the infinitive, not the participle, after another infinitive (hat das Auto reparieren lassen)
WORDS verb.ersatz: lassen | sehen | hören | helfen
# NAMES: people, as subjects and owners (Anna kommt; Peters Auto)
# ... and the fixed place phrases (spoken as written here)
# (... and no zu-infinitive of them: not "beginnt zu stürzen", "versucht, sich zu befinden")
# the verbs whose prepositional object may be a da-word and a clause (Ich freue mich darauf, dass du kommst; Er denkt
# daran, das Licht auszumachen) -- not "zögert damit, dass die Hütte liegt"

# (the sentence's shapes -- S -> MAIN "," SUB "." ... -- are the world's: world/shapes.txt)

# ---- the clause types, field by field -------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# main clause: one part in front, the finite verb second, the rest of the verb at the end
MAIN -> FRONT vfin MIDDLE vrest clause
# after a subordinate clause in front: the verb first (Weil es regnet, bleibe ich zu Hause.)
INVERTED -> vfin MIDDLE vrest clause
# subordinate clause: the conjunction, the finite verb last (..., dass er den Apfel gegessen hat)
SUB -> conj MIDDLE vrest vfin clause
# relative clause, in a noun phrase: the relative pronoun is the role it stands for (..., der den Apfel isst, ...)
REL -> "," relpron MIDDLE vrest vfin ","
# questions: yes/no -- the verb first; a question word -- in front, the verb second
YESNO -> vfin MIDDLE vrest clause
WH -> wh vfin MIDDLE vrest clause
# imperative: the verb first (the polite form keeps its subject: Essen Sie ...)
IMP -> vfin MIDDLE vrest clause
# a zu-infinitive, an object of the clause before: no subject of its own, the zu-infinitive last
# (Er versucht, das Buch zu lesen; Sie erlaubt ihm, früher anzufangen); with um, ohne, statt: an adverbial one
ZU -> MIDDLE vzu
ZUADV -> conj MIDDLE vzu
# reported speech: a main clause's order, its verb in the Konjunktiv I (Er sagt, er sei krank)
REP -> FRONT vfin MIDDLE vrest clause
# a second main clause with the first one's subject left out (Er kauft Brot und trinkt Kaffee)
DROP -> vfin MIDDLE vrest clause
# je ... desto: a comparative in front of each (Je länger er arbeitet, desto müder wird er)
JE -> w.je jecomp MIDDLE vrest vfin
DESTO -> w.desto pred vfin MIDDLE vrest
# a condition without wenn: the verb first (Hätte ich Zeit, würde ich kommen)
CONDV -> vfin MIDDLE vrest
# wer ..., (der) ...: who does it, (he) ... -- the main clause after it with der as its subject (Wer Hunger hat, der isst)
WERREL -> relpron MIDDLE vrest vfin

# the front field: the subject mostly, sometimes a time or a comment, now and then the object
FRONT -> subj                                {7}
FRONT -> adv.time                            {2}
FRONT -> adv.sentence                        {1}
FRONT -> adv.conn                            {4}
FRONT -> front.A                             {1}
FRONT -> adv.gen                             {1}   @cause-phrases
FRONT -> front.pp                            {0}
FRONT -> adv.manner                          {0}
FRONT -> adv.role                            {0}
# (weight 0: never drawn -- only a story's front=pp / front=manner / front=role asks for them: Mit einem Löffel ...,
#  Langsam ..., Als Koch ...)

# (part: a modal particle -- doch, ja, mal, denn, bitte; fixed: a fixed word -- es tut mir leid; adv.gen: a reason or a
#  concession with the genitive -- wegen des Regens, trotz der Kälte)
# (obj.D.new, obj.A.new: an object that is NEW -- indefinite, with kein, with no article -- after the time and the place:
#  Ich kaufe heute keinen Apfel; Man spricht hier Deutsch; Ich gebe dem Kind ein Buch -- a known one before them)
# the middle field, in its usual order: the subject (after pronoun objects when it is a noun: Passiert uns ein Unfall?),
# the pronoun objects (accusative before dative: gibt es ihm; the
# reflexive among them: stellt es sich vor), a comment (außerdem, leider), the noun objects (dative before accusative;
# a second accusative after the first: lehrt die Kinder Deutsch, nennt den Mann einen Lügner), then time,
# the negation, frequency (nicht immer), manner, the prepositional object and place (`nicht` before what it negates), last a
# predicative adjective (Der Apfel ist nicht rot)
MIDDLE -> pron.A.pre pron.D.pre refl.pre subj pron.A refl pron.D adv.sentence adv.conn adv.role part obj.D obj.A obj.A2 obj.G adv.time adv.gen adv.manner.pre neg adv.freq adv.manner adv.place obj.D.new obj.A.new obj.bare pobj pred fixed

# ---- phrases ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# (attr: an owner after the noun -- das Auto meines Vaters, ein Freund von mir)
NP -> det adj noun attr rel
# a prepositional phrase: the preposition takes the noun phrase's definite article in where German folds them (im, zum)
PP -> prep det adj noun attr rel
